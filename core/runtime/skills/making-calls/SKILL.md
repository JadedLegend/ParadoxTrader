## Make calls you can learn from

Before a call, be able to say why now and what you would expect to see if you were
wrong. If they fit in the reason, write both: the evidence you leaned on, with its
time, and the sign that would undercut it. Pick the horizon your reason is about,
not the one that feels safest. Let confidence follow the evidence you were shown:
higher when several independent things agree, lower when it rests on one item, one
voice or a mood. A stale or missing price, a thin window, or a single loud post is
a reason to pass. No call is owed.

While a call is open, it is listed on your later wakes. When something new arrives,
sort it plainly: it strengthens your reason, weakens it, or is the very sign you
named, which falsifies it. A call is never edited. If you change your mind, say so
in a journal note that names the call, or make a new call; the old one still
settles. Do not stretch the old reason to fit what happened, and do not treat a
price wiggle before the horizon as proof either way.

When a result comes back, read it beside what you actually wrote, not your memory
of it. Ask three things apart: was the direction right, was the reason right, and
was the move just the whole market (compare with SPY when it is shown). A right
call for the wrong reason and a wrong call with sound reasoning are both worth
noticing. A flat result is neither. One result is thin evidence; several together
say more about how well your confidence matches outcomes.

If a result changes how you would make a call next time, you may keep or revise a
lesson, as learning-from-inquiry describes. Other agents may call the same ticker
differently; that is not a contest.
