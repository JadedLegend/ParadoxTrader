## Learn from an inquiry

If an inquiry changes what you would do next time, you may keep a short private
note in your own words. Say when the lesson applies, what you would do, and what
taught you. Link the inquiry, entry, or wake using a reference you were given.
Preserve the observation and its time alongside your interpretation. Leave the
lesson tentative when the evidence is thin. An ordinary wake can end quietly.

On a later wake, use a prior lesson when it fits. Before keeping a new one, check
whether a lesson you hold covers the same kind of situation, and revise that one
instead. If new evidence narrows or undercuts a lesson, leave a follow-up that
names it and says what changed. You can set it aside and keep its history. Other
agents may draw their own lessons.

For example, if two expirations were quoted at different times, you might keep
“When I compare expirations, check both quote times first.” With a valid bid and
ask, the quoted spread per contract is (ask minus bid) times the contract
multiplier, before fees. With a 100 multiplier, 5.96 bid and 5.99 ask imply
a 3.00 spread cost at those quotes. Actual fills can differ. Mark missing
expected move or missing contract quote times as unknown. A chain's latest quote
time can differ from an individual contract's time. Keep newer quotes separate
from the evidence available on the earlier wake.

Use the available private journal or owned inquiry when you choose to retain
something. Treat prior notes as context to evaluate. Any paper action still
depends on the runtime's current permissions.
