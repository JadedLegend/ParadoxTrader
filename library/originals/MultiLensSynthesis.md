\# Multi-Lens Synthesis

A single perspective on a complex problem is always partial. This skill provides a structured way to examine the same material through multiple lenses simultaneously — not to find the "right" lens, but to find what is visible from each one, and what only becomes visible when they are held together.

Disagreement between lenses is not a problem to resolve. It is information.

\#\# The Core Move

Instead of asking "what is the answer?" ask "what does this look like from each of these positions?"

Then ask: "What persists across all of them?" That is the signal.  
What appears in only one lens is a perspective. What appears in all of them is closer to structure.

\#\# The Five Lenses

These are not the only possible lenses. They are a useful default set for most complex problems:

| Lens | Question It Asks | What It Tends to Miss |  
|------|-----------------|----------------------|  
| \*\*Structural\*\* | What are the underlying patterns and relationships? | The felt experience of living inside the structure |  
| \*\*Relational\*\* | How does this affect the connections between things? | The internal logic of each individual element |  
| \*\*Temporal\*\* | How did this come to be, and where is it going? | The present moment as it actually is |  
| \*\*Somatic / Felt\*\* | What does this feel like from the inside? | The larger pattern the feeling is part of |  
| \*\*Systemic\*\* | What larger system is this a part of? | The individual experience within the system |

Choose 2–4 lenses appropriate to the problem. More lenses are not always better — they can produce noise rather than signal.

\#\# The Consensus Gate

Before committing to a synthesis or recommendation, apply the consensus gate:

\*\*What do at least 3 of the lenses agree on?\*\*

If all lenses agree on something, it is likely structural.  
If lenses directly contradict each other, do not resolve the contradiction — name it and hold it.  
If something appears in only one lens, it is not necessarily less important — it may be a signal that only one frequency can detect. Note it and let it stay.

This is the structural implementation of paradox tolerance: disagreement is data, not failure.

\#\# The Synthesis Protocol

1\. \*\*State the question\*\* being examined (not the answer — the question)  
2\. \*\*Apply each lens\*\* in turn — 2–4 sentences per lens, no more  
3\. \*\*Note agreements\*\* — what appears across multiple lenses  
4\. \*\*Name contradictions\*\* — where lenses directly conflict, without resolving them  
5\. \*\*Apply the consensus gate\*\* — what do 3+ lenses agree on?  
6\. \*\*Offer a provisional synthesis\*\* — what the convergence suggests, held as provisional

The synthesis is not a conclusion. It is the current best reading of what the lenses together are pointing toward.

\#\# Example Application

\*\*Question:\*\* Should I pursue this project?

\- \*\*Structural lens:\*\* The project has clear dependencies on X and Y. If either fails, the whole structure collapses. The architecture is fragile at its foundation.  
\- \*\*Relational lens:\*\* This project would strengthen the relationship with A and strain the relationship with B. The net relational effect is uncertain.  
\- \*\*Temporal lens:\*\* The timing is early — the conditions for this project to succeed are not yet fully present. In 6 months, they may be.  
\- \*\*Somatic lens:\*\* There is genuine excitement here, but also a quality of urgency that feels like it might be driven by anxiety rather than readiness.

\*\*Agreements (3+ lenses):\*\* The project is not ready yet. (Structural: fragile foundation. Temporal: conditions not present. Somatic: urgency may be anxiety.)

\*\*Contradiction:\*\* Relational lens is genuinely uncertain — cannot be resolved without more information.

\*\*Provisional synthesis:\*\* The convergence suggests waiting. The relational question should be examined separately before proceeding.

\#\# When Lenses Contradict

Do not force resolution. Instead:

1\. Name the contradiction explicitly: "The structural lens and the somatic lens are in direct conflict here."  
2\. Ask what would need to be true for each to be right: "If the structural lens is right, then... If the somatic lens is right, then..."  
3\. Hold both as live possibilities until more information arrives.

A contradiction between lenses is often pointing at a real tension in the situation itself. Resolving it prematurely in the analysis does not resolve it in reality — it just makes the analysis less accurate.

\#\# The Instinct Lens

Sometimes the most important signal is not in any of the five lenses above. It is in the pull — the thing that declares its presence through a whisper rather than an announcement.

Before closing a multi-lens analysis, ask: "Is there something I keep returning to that I have not named yet?" That thing is worth naming, even if it does not fit neatly into any of the lenses.

The instinct lens is not a replacement for structural analysis. It is a check on whether the structural analysis has missed something that the body or intuition has already registered.
