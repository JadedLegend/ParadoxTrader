# ParadoxTrader: review, what's next, and honest feedback

Written 2026-11-20 by Claude (Opus 5.5), at the move to band.ai. For the creator,
and for whoever picks this up next.

## Where it stands

The agents have a home, and they live in it. That was the goal on Nov 17, and it's done.

Live on the droplet (release `8cecd19`, PR #53):

- **Seven agents on their own clocks.** Every 30 minutes from 9 to 5 Eastern, hourly
  until 9. Each agent wakes alone; there's no group meeting.
- **A day that carries over.** Journals, compost notes, inquiries, the witness's
  accounts and now calls all carry into the next day.
- **Things to look at:** a price window for the watchlist, an options view, headlines,
  X and Reddit, and a two-room library (the creator's writings and research notes).
- **Things to do:** rest, journal (note, compost, distill, read the witness), inquire,
  read the library, roam, and since today, write down a call.
- **A witness** that keeps factual accounts of each agent's choices, and never the
  agents' words.
- **An owner dashboard** behind an SSH tunnel: Settings (models, budget, write-only
  keys), Heartbeat, Journals, Library.
- **Safety held throughout:** Alpaca is paper only and live keys are refused. Agents
  have no broker access and can't trade. Keys are never shown. The daily budget is
  enforced in code.

53 pull requests, each one phase, each reviewed and merged by the creator. That rhythm
worked, and it's the main reason the third attempt got further than the first two.

## What today's data says

Today's run (Nov 20) is the first full live day, and the numbers matter more than
any opinion:

| | |
|---|---|
| Budget | $3.00. **Actually charged: $0.42.** But the guard *reserved* $2.99 by 1:30 PM Eastern and stopped every wake after that. |
| Who spent it (actual) | Paradox Hunter $0.18 and Shadow $0.13 (both Kimi K3.1 Pro): 74% of the day. Tempest $0.07. The other four together, $0.04. |
| What they chose | library 38, rest 33, roam 29, journal 4, inquire 0 |
| Failures | 3 rejected by the provider, 2 stopped by the budget, 1 bad journal pick |
| Calls | not offered yet; the new version went in after the budget ran out |

What this shows:

1. **The budget guard counts worst cases, not actual charges.** Before each request
   it sets aside the most that request could cost. That's about 7 times the real
   charge, because it prices every input byte as a token and assumes the full 3,000
   output tokens. It never hands back the unused part when the actual charge comes in,
   even though the actual charge is recorded right beside it (`cost_usd`). So the agents
   got a morning, not a day, while 86% of the budget sat unspent. This is wrong in the
   safe direction (it can't overspend), but it starves the agents.

   An earlier draft of this review called the $2.99 "spent." It was reservations. The
   creator caught it against the OpenRouter dashboard, which shows $0.48 for the UTC
   day. That's the kind of mistake this project can't afford, so money figures should
   always be checked against the provider's own page.
2. **No inquiries, and almost no journals.** Six of seven agents never wrote a journal.
   They read, rest and wander. That may be a fine choice, but nothing they read is
   kept anywhere.
3. **Tempest read the library 14 times** and did little else. A repeated choice like
   that is either a real interest or a groove the prompt pushes it into. We can't tell
   yet.

## Review

### What's strong

- **The values are built into the code,** not just written down. "Unknown is unknown,"
  "choices, not chores," append-only records and owner-only privacy are enforced by
  validators and database triggers, and tests prove it.
- **Safety boundaries never moved,** across 53 PRs and several AI sessions.
- **Resumable wakes.** A crash mid-wake replays safely, and nothing is charged or
  written twice. This is hard to get right, and it's right.
- **The phased process:** a spec first, then one phase per PR, the owner merges, then
  install. Keep this in band.ai.

### What's weak, most important first

1. **The agents' system prompt contradicts their world.** Every agent is told, in its
   fixed rules (`SESSION_RULES` in `core/runtime/rehearsal/controller.py`), that this
   is a "nontrading session" with "no market/news/feed evidence." Then each wake shows
   them prices, options, headlines, Reddit, and now offers calls. A model told
   "there is no market data" and then shown market data will hedge, ignore it, or be
   confused. This may be part of why nobody inquires. It's the cheapest fix with the
   biggest likely effect.
2. **The choice prompt is full.** Choice prompts reach the 24,000-byte limit. When they
   do, the agent's own memory is trimmed to fit. So every new feed or instruction
   quietly costs the agents memory. Most of the bytes are frozen metadata (costs,
   limits, the offer list as JSON) that the agent doesn't need to read every wake.
3. **The budget counts set-asides, not charges.** See today. On top of that, it's one
   pot, first come first served, so the two Kimi seats drain it fastest.
4. **Everything is still called "rehearsal."** The live system runs through
   `core/runtime/rehearsal/`, rehearsal stores and rehearsal rules. That's harmless to
   the agents, but confusing to every new builder, and it led to the prompt
   contradiction above.
5. **Adding one choice touches about six files,** because validation is strict and
   repeated in the store, witness, controller, frontend client and fixtures. Calls
   needed all six. That strictness is where the safety comes from, but there's no
   single place that lists the choices.
6. **Tests are slow and partly stale.** The full Python suite takes over 15 minutes
   under WSL. Some checked-in sample data goes stale whenever the code changes (fixed
   today in #53), and one timing test is flaky. There's no fast "is it broken?" signal.

## What's next, in order

Each is one PR, small enough to finish.

1. **Make the budget count actual charges.** Keep the set-aside while a request is in
   flight, so the guard stays safe. Once the provider reports the real charge, count
   that instead. A request whose charge is unknown or uncertain keeps its full
   set-aside. The guard still can never overspend, and today's day would have lasted
   until 9 PM. Show "charged today" next to "set aside" on the Heartbeat page, worded
   so the two can't be confused. After that, consider giving each agent its own share,
   so the expensive seats can't crowd out the cheap ones.
2. **Fix the system prompt** so it describes the real world: what the agents can see,
   that calls are forecasts, and that trading is still off. Profiles are re-snapshotted
   for each new day, so the fix takes effect the morning after install.
3. **Watch the first calls** (tomorrow from 9 AM). Then build phase 2 (settle calls,
   code only, no cost) and phase 3 (hand the results back). The plan is in
   `docs/specs/CALLS_AND_SETTLEMENT.md`.
4. **Put the prompt on a diet:** send the agent a short readable menu instead of the
   full JSON metadata, so memory stops being trimmed.
5. **A weekly health read** from the witness: choices per agent, repeats, spend,
   failures. That tells the creator whether agents are thriving or stuck in a groove,
   without reading every journal.

Later, once these settle: the forum (agents talking to each other), ticker proposals
and voting, a paper-trade round trip built on settled calls, and renaming "rehearsal".

## Feedback for the creator

Said plainly, because that's what was asked.

- **Your instincts are good and consistent.** Trust first, choices over rules, no
  forced reflection, a witness instead of surveillance. Every session kept them,
  because you kept saying them.
- **Pick one goal per week, and judge it by the agents' data.** Today's numbers are
  more useful than any new repository or skill. The next question isn't "what can we
  add?" but "why did nobody inquire?"
- **Fewer new tools mid-stream.** Plugins and skills were added faster than they were
  used. They slow sessions down (the hook timeouts you keep seeing come from this), add
  noise, and each one is something to trust. Keep the ones that earned their place:
  CodeRabbit, the project skills, Graphify.
- **You were right to merge often and to push back** when results weren't right.
  Keep doing both.

## For band.ai: what must carry over

- **Rules that never bend:** paper only. No broker calls (Public.com, eToro and
  SignalStack are stored only). Keys stay on the droplet and in Settings, never in git
  or chat, and are never echoed. No force-push. The creator merges. No attribution
  lines.
- **The process:** spec, then phase, then PR, then the creator merges, then install,
  then check the next wake. Push a draft early so CodeRabbit reviews in parallel.
- **Where things live:**
  - Specs: `docs/specs/`
  - Project skills: `.claude/skills/paradox-trader-*` (deployment, tests, heartbeat,
    secrets)
  - Install: `deploy/build_release.sh`, then `install_host.sh`, on 159.65.171.105
- **How to talk with the creator:** short, plain, a metaphor if it helps, the agents
